# Bed Bug Treatment

## Identification
Adults are apple-seed sized, flat, reddish-brown; nymphs are smaller and pale. Evidence: rust-colored fecal spotting on mattress seams and box springs, shed skins, blood spots on sheets, live bugs in seams, tufts, and headboard crevices, and bites in lines or clusters. Confirm with a physical specimen or clear photo evidence — bites alone are not a confirmation.

## Treatment Steps
1. Full inspection: mattress seams, box spring, bed frame joints, headboard, nightstands, baseboards, outlet covers, and upholstered furniture within 10 ft of the bed.
2. Document findings with photos; grade the infestation (light / moderate / heavy).
3. Have the customer complete the prep list below — treatment fails without prep.
4. Steam-treat seams, tufts, cracks, and crevices at contact pace.
5. Apply targeted insecticide applications per label instructions to cracks, crevices, and voids (product list pending approval).
6. Install encasements on mattress and box spring; place interceptors under all bed and sofa legs.
7. Schedule the follow-up before leaving — single-visit bed bug jobs fail.

## Customer Prep
- Wash ALL bedding and clothing from affected rooms; dry on high heat; store in sealed bags.
- Reduce clutter around beds; do not move items to other rooms (spreads the infestation).
- Vacuum mattress seams, bed frame, and carpet edges; discard the vacuum bag outside in a sealed bag.
- Pull beds 6 inches off walls; keep bedding from touching the floor.
- Do not self-treat with foggers — they scatter bed bugs deeper into walls.

## Follow-up
- Re-inspect and re-treat at the interval the product label requires (typically 2 weeks — confirm per label).
- Check interceptors at each visit; continue until two consecutive clean inspections.
- Keep encasements on for at least a year.

## Safety
- Mattresses and couches are sensitive treatment sites — only label-approved products and sites, no exceptions.
- Customer and pets out of treated rooms until dry / per label re-entry.
- Wear label-required PPE.
